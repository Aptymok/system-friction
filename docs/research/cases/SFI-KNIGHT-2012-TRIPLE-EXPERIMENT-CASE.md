### Why this matters

**No substitution of identity:**
- Knight Capital the company is **not** CT-A01 or another agent-lineage subject.
- An event that took place in 2012 is **not** the unrevealed naturalistic target of a freeze in 2026.
- A genuine historical source does not certify an inference about a particular person's intent.
- A regulatory finding is documentary evidence of what a regulator determined, not SFI first-hand instrument telemetry.
- If the model has read the known incident postmortem or learned it in pretraining, its present-day 'forecast' of the outcome **cannot** be represented as a blind 2012 prediction.

## Three source-vintage snapshots: ONE case, three analyses

The executable source selector uses a conservative **whole-day publication-time** rule. It will not pretend to know the intra-day publication hour of a filing.

1. `asOfLocal=2012-08-01`: before the incident, public documents can establish NYSE's RLP regulatory context. They **cannot** provide the later SEC findings about Knight's eighth server, emails, rollback or loss. All three tracks must surface this evidence gap.
2. `asOfLocal=2012-08-03`: the August 2 Knight company disclosure becomes available. Detailed SEC 2013 incident mechanics remain inadmissible to a public 2012 replay.
3. `asOfLocal=2013-10-17`: the SEC 2013 order can support a *retrospective* detailed reading of Power Peg and rollback. No resulting computation can be called a 2012 blind forecast.

A real organization's internal pre-incident knowledge would require its actual contemporaneous internal records. Public-release cutoffs **do not substitute for internal actor knowledge**; where that distinction matters, mark `UNKNOWN`.
