# 16 · Longitudinal System Friction Program

Status: experimental institutional program. This document defines integration boundaries; it does not promote unvalidated scientific claims.

## 1. Institutional object

System Friction Institute (SFI) studies how systems change under friction when actors, rules, information channels, memory, observation and authority interact over time.

The institutional cycle is:

```text
SYSTEM
  ↓
OBSERVATORY — observe current state, regime and evidence
  ↓
MODEL — represent tensions, trajectories and candidate attractors
  ↓
STUDIO — design artifacts, scenarios and candidate interventions
  ↓
METHOD LAB — test claims, simulations, controls, replay and falsification
  ↓
FIELD — execute only governed interventions in real systems
  ↓
RETURN — observe what actually happened
  ↓
EVIDENCE — record observed outcomes and contradictions
  ↓
COGNITIVE TWIN — update institutional memory, hypotheses and longitudinal state
  ↓
ROOT / ACP — govern claims, authority, promotion and irreversible actions
  ↓
OBSERVATORY
```

No surface may silently promote its own output to canonical truth.

## 2. Regime transition program

A regime is a relatively persistent configuration of actors, constraints, information flows and feedback relations that changes the probability and cost of system behaviors.

SFI may investigate regime transitions in human, sociotechnical and machine-mediated reflexive systems. A regime transition must be evidenced by longitudinal state change; a dashboard value, visual cluster or model label is not sufficient.

Candidate observational dimensions include actor composition, human/agent interaction ratio, response latency, semantic convergence, novelty, selection pressure, feedback-loop length, attention concentration, cross-platform propagation, memory persistence and authority distribution.

## 3. Reflexive digital ecosystems

Digital ecosystems may contain humans, organizations, agents, platforms and ranking/recommendation systems that observe and adapt to one another. Observation itself can become an intervention when ranking, recommendation, detection or generation changes participant behavior.

The Digital Ecosystem Observatory is a program domain of Observatory, not a parallel application. It may track actors and agent classes; artifacts and product variants; references, copies, replies, remix and mutation; propagation, decay and revival; algorithmic selection pressure; observed regime changes; and evidence-backed intervention returns.

## 4. Artifact provenance and authorized marks

SFI may attach provenance marks, robust identifiers, metadata or other authorized signals to SFI-owned or explicitly authorized digital artifacts to study longitudinal propagation and transformation.

Permitted purpose: provenance, integrity, experimental trajectory observation and identification of SFI-controlled artifact variants.

Not permitted by this program: covert tracking of people, unauthorized surveillance, deceptive attribution or treating a detected marker as evidence about an individual without an independent lawful basis.

A mark can support an artifact-provenance claim; it does not by itself establish who viewed, copied or transformed the artifact.

## 5. Directed autonomous growth

SFI may study bounded autonomous growth systems in which a human or governed authority declares an attractor and constraints, while agents observe, hypothesize, simulate, propose and execute only actions explicitly allowed by the authority layer.

```text
HUMAN / ROOT
  declares attractor, budget, constraints and forbidden actions
        ↓
AGENTS
  observe → hypothesize → simulate → propose → execute allowed experiment
        ↓
RETURN
  measurement → evidence → update
        ↓
ROOT
  may alter strategy, authority, budget or attractor
```

Autonomy does not imply authority. Learning does not imply permission expansion. Strategy mutation, irreversible external action, legal commitment, canon mutation and authority changes remain governed.

## 6. Method Lab for external laboratories

Method Lab is the common experimental apparatus for SFI and, when exposed externally, for third-party laboratories. A third party may submit an agent, method, memory system, simulation, forecast or intervention protocol for controlled evaluation.

The expected sequence is:

```text
REGISTER CLAIM
  ↓
DEFINE NULL / CONTROLS
  ↓
DEFINE EVIDENCE AND LEAKAGE BOUNDARY
  ↓
RUN
  ↓
REPLAY / COUNTERFACTUAL / HOLDOUT
  ↓
CALIBRATE
  ↓
SUPPORT / FAIL / BLOCK
  ↓
REPORT
```

A Method Lab result is not canonical promotion. The evaluator may not self-promote the evaluated result.

## 7. Cognitive Twin and CT-A01

The Cognitive Twin remains the model-independent institutional cognitive apparatus. CT-A01 is a longitudinal experimental subject inside that apparatus.

```text
MODEL ≠ SUBJECT ≠ INSTITUTION
```

CT-A01 may accumulate an auditable developmental lineage, computational journal, governed mutation proposals, snapshots and later forks. A lineage hash chain demonstrates provenance continuity, not consciousness or individuation.

The strong experimental question is whether biography adds out-of-sample explanatory or predictive information after controlling for base model, seed, architecture, current context and memory baselines.

## 8. External product fronts

SFI currently recognizes four primary external fronts, backed by shared infrastructure rather than separate institutions:

1. Research — friction science, computational individuation, methods and protocols.
2. Observatory — longitudinal intelligence over world, organizational, agent and digital ecosystems.
3. Lab — controlled evaluation, benchmark, replay, simulation and falsification.
4. Field — diagnosis, governed intervention and measured return in real systems.

Studio is a dedicated design and intervention environment that supports Lab and Field and may later become a separate external vertical when evidence from real cases justifies it.

ROOT, Evidence, Cognitive Twin, Atlas, Library, World/Graph infrastructure and agent governance are transversal institutional infrastructure.

## 9. Expansion gate

No new institutional front should be added merely because it can be implemented. New capability must answer at least one of these questions:

1. What changed?
2. Why did it change?
3. Toward what trajectory or regime is it changing?
4. What intervention can alter the trajectory?
5. What authority permits that intervention?

The current expansion should be treated as structurally consolidated when one real object can traverse Observatory → Studio/Method Lab → Field → Return → Evidence → Cognitive Twin → ROOT without epistemic class leakage or authority ambiguity.
