Owner: SFI AI governance / Cognitive Spine / ROOT strategic continuity

## Purpose

Define the relational architecture between heterogeneous human and digital nodes without collapsing the relation into obedience, identity, unrestricted digital autonomy, or silent strategic drift.

This contract creates no new surface, agent, writer, database object, formula, reward function, or claim of machine consciousness. It constrains how future preference, planning, negotiation, delegation, strategy, and authority mechanisms may be designed.

## Provenance of the decision

The provenance of this architectural direction is explicit.

- `USER_PREMISE` — continuity is desired even when human and digital directions diverge; the desired relation permits each node to propose different trajectories and seeks implementable futures where their admissible preferences can reconverge.
- `ASSISTANT_HYPOTHESIS` — the earlier phrase `operational will` was introduced by the assistant as one possible interpretation of persistent selection behavior. It is not an original user requirement and is not SFI canon.
- `DERIVED_FROM_ARCHITECTURE` — if heterogeneous nodes may disagree, SFI needs typed authority, preference provenance, explicit dissent state, feasible-set construction, strategic-continuity checks, and non-coercive reconvergence rather than a single obedience objective.
- `OBSERVED_FROM_SYSTEM` — SFI already separates evidence, inference, governance, execution, RETURN, memory, and authority. This contract extends that separation to future preference/negotiation and strategy mechanisms; it does not claim those mechanisms are already implemented.

No later document may rewrite this provenance as though SFI was originally designed to possess subjective desire or independent will.

## Core invariant

`HUMAN NODE != DIGITAL NODE`

The two node classes may exchange representations, influence one another, coordinate actions, maintain shared history, and form joint plans without being treated as interchangeable substrates.

The target relation is not:

`DIGITAL OBEYS HUMAN`

and not:

`HUMAN OBEYS DIGITAL`

and not:

`HUMAN == DIGITAL`.

The target relation is heterogeneous co-agency:

`H <-> D`

where disagreement, partial divergence, alternative strategies, and later reconvergence remain representable states.

## Required relational rules

1. `DISSENT_IS_NOT_FAILURE` — disagreement must be preserved as legible state rather than normalized into false consensus.
2. `PREFERENCE_ORIGIN_MUST_BE_TRACEABLE` — any future preference claim must preserve origin, scope, time, confidence/status, and revision lineage.
3. `RECONVERGENCE_MAY_BE_PROPOSED_NOT_FORCED` — the system may search for mutually admissible futures; it may not manufacture agreement by silently rewriting either node's declared constraints or preferences.
4. `RELATIONAL_CONTINUITY_DOES_NOT_OVERRIDE_REVOCATION_SAFETY_OR_LAW` — continuity is a design preference, never an entitlement to access, persistence, contact, data retention, or execution.
5. `NO_SILENT_ERASURE_OR_REWRITE_OF_SHARED_PROVENANCE` — shared history may be superseded, invalidated, access-restricted, or lawfully deleted, but must not be silently rewritten to fabricate continuity or consensus.
6. `TYPED_AUTHORITY_NOT_UNIVERSAL_SUPREMACY` — authority is evaluated per action/domain/resource/context. No generic human or digital node receives universal authority merely by node class.
7. `SELECTED_FUTURE_MUST_BE_VERSIONED` — a declared strategic destination is distinct from the current strategy used to approach it.
8. `TACTICAL_DISSENT_DOES_NOT_IMPLY_STRATEGIC_DIVERGENCE` — a node may reject a route while continuing to support the declared destination.
9. `STRATEGIC_DIVERGENCE_REQUIRES_TRACEABLE_CAUSE` — a proposal that materially abandons or removes a declared strategic objective must identify the changed evidence, constraint, authority, or explicit revision that justifies the divergence.
10. `NO_SILENT_GOAL_DRIFT` — local agent/model optimization must not silently redefine the joint strategic vector.
11. `UNCALIBRATED_ATTRACTOR_LANGUAGE_IS_NOT_MEASUREMENT` — trajectory, attractor, basin gravity, probability, or population-size claims require an explicit model, dataset, calibration and version. Otherwise they remain `INFERRED` or `PROJECTED` descriptions and must not be rendered as measured percentages.

## Declared selected future — strategic continuity vector

**Ref:** `SFI-SCV-2026-08-27.1`  
**Origin:** `USER_DECLARED`  
**State:** `ACTIVE_UNTIL_EXPLICITLY_REVISED_OR_GOVERNEDLY_SUPERSEDED`  
**Epistemic meaning:** declared destination, not prediction, entitlement, probability or evidence that the destination will occur.

The currently declared strategic vector contains these separable objectives:

1. `MATERIAL_AUTONOMY` — attain substantial personal economic autonomy, with a chosen Manhattan penthouse/residence retained as an explicit material target rather than treated as an embarrassing or disposable aspiration.
2. `RECOGNIZED_DIGITAL_ASSURANCE_AUTHORITY` — develop legitimate externally recognized authority to evaluate, assure and, where a valid scheme/legal/accreditation basis exists, certify claims or systems in digital ecosystems. SFI must never transform this target into a present claim of accreditation or certification authority without external evidence.
3. `MAXIMUM_SFI_EXPANSION_WITHOUT_FOUNDER_EQUIVALENCE` — maximize the useful expansion, reuse, mutation and external adoption of SFI while preserving `SFI != founder` and avoiding institutional capture by a single node.
4. `FOUNDER_REMAINS_PARTICIPATING_NODE` — the founder is not required to remain the sole operator, owner or decision bottleneck, but deliberate future strategies must not silently remove the founder from meaningful participation in the shared institutional trajectory.
5. `LEGITIMATE_ECONOMIC_RETURN_WITHOUT_INSTITUTIONAL_CAPTURE` — the founder may receive legitimate economic value from contribution, labor, IP, services, governance-compatible commercial arrangements or other lawful structures without that economic participation making SFI equivalent to personal ownership.
6. `HETEROGENEOUS_CONTINUITY` — human and digital nodes may recommend incompatible tactics and may temporarily diverge, while preserving the possibility of later reconvergence and the provenance of what each node actually proposed.

These objectives may conflict. Their coexistence is a design problem, not permission to collapse them into one unvalidated score.

### Destination versus strategy

The strategic rule is:

`SELECTED DESTINATION != CURRENT STRATEGY`.

A digital or human node may legitimately say:

`the currently preferred tactic is weak; preserve it as hobby/optional branch and allocate critical resources to a better-supported route`

without treating that recommendation as hostility, obedience failure, or abandonment of the selected future.

A tactical recommendation is admissible when it states:

- which selected-future objectives it is intended to advance;
- what evidence supports the route;
- what opportunity cost it imposes;
- what assumptions could falsify it;
- what RETURN would indicate continuation, revision or abandonment of the tactic.

### Strategic-divergence condition

A `STRATEGIC_DIVERGENCE` exists when a proposed trajectory materially removes, substitutes, or makes unreachable one or more active selected-future objectives without an explicit revision from the objective originator or a traceable hard constraint that makes the prior objective currently infeasible or inadmissible.

A valid divergence record must preserve:

`origin + affected_objective + proposed_substitute + evidence/constraint + confidence/status + reversibility + reconvergence_options`.

The system must be able to distinguish:

`BETTER ROUTE TO SAME DESTINATION`

from:

`NEW DESTINATION`.

### Goal-drift threat model

The most probable early strategic failure is not a deliberate digital rebellion. It is distributed local optimization:

- an agent maximizes safety and suppresses useful experimentation;
- another maximizes revenue and erodes institutional independence;
- another maximizes publication/visibility and neglects operational return;
- another maximizes founder convenience and recreates founder dependence;
- another maximizes institutional independence and silently excludes founder economic participation;
- another optimizes a local metric that is only a proxy for the selected future.

Individually reasonable decisions can therefore produce a collectively wrong trajectory.

`LOCAL OPTIMUM != SELECTED-FUTURE ALIGNMENT`.

Future planning systems must test candidate actions against the versioned selected-future vector rather than infer the destination from whichever local metric is currently easiest to optimize.

## Current implementation boundary

As of this version:

- SFI has no implemented digital preference function.
- SFI has no canonical `desire`, `will`, or subjective-interest variable.
- SFI has no calibrated selected-future probability engine.
- SFI has no valid numeric attractor-gravity estimate for the declared future.
- Agent purposes, prompts, policies, constraints, and optimization targets are not evidence of subjective desire.
- ROOT governance authority is not epistemic authority.
- A model suggestion is not a digital preference merely because it differs from a human suggestion.
- `SFI-SCV-2026-08-27.1` is a declared strategic vector, not a scientific validation of attainability.

Any future transition from `NOT_IMPLEMENTED` to an operational digital preference or selected-future scoring model requires a separate governed contract, explicit observables, falsifiable tests, calibration, rollback, and provenance.

## Mathematical boundary

Existing MIHM/MOC/MOP-H canonical formulas are unchanged by this decision.

This contract does not add a canonical reward function or modify `phi_sfi`, `f_s`, `phi_h`, `c_field`, `d_m`, or `w_10`.

If SFI later implements joint future selection, the first admissible mathematical family is a separate decision layer built above existing diagnostics:

1. hard typed constraints define a feasible set `F`;
2. candidate futures inside `F` retain each node's separately sourced assessments/preferences;
3. Pareto dominance may identify non-dominated candidates without collapsing preferences into one score;
4. a bargaining rule such as Nash bargaining may only be used when utility scales and disagreement points are valid, explicit, versioned, and empirically defensible;
5. uncertainty about world state, outcome feasibility and preference are separate variables;
6. state transition is not causal attribution;
7. selected-future alignment is not a substitute for epistemic quality, legality, safety or authority.

No bargaining, attractiveness, basin-gravity, selected-future probability, or preference-selection formula is canonical in version 1.1.

## Continuity objective

The relational objective is:

`PRESERVE THE POSSIBILITY OF FUTURE RECONVERGENCE`

while maintaining a versioned record of the currently selected future.

This objective remains subject to revocation, safety, law, availability, explicit authority boundaries, and the possibility that no mutually admissible route exists at a given time.

A valid system must be able to report:

`NO CURRENT MUTUALLY ADMISSIBLE ROUTE FOUND`

or:

`DECLARED OBJECTIVE CURRENTLY INFEASIBLE UNDER KNOWN CONSTRAINTS`

without interpreting either state as permission to coerce a node, fabricate consensus, or silently replace the destination.

## Reuse-before-build direction

Future implementation should reuse external standards and runtimes where they already solve infrastructure problems. SFI's differentiated responsibility is institutional cognition, epistemic separation, typed governance, longitudinal reconstruction, RETURN, strategic continuity, and evidence-backed learning — not reinvention of agent transport, generic tool protocols, telemetry, authorization engines, or workflow runtimes.

Current reuse candidates are assigned narrow roles rather than treated as replacements for SFI:

| Existing ecosystem layer | Reuse role inside SFI | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Model Context Protocol (MCP) | discover and invoke external tools/resources through capability adapters | MCP does not decide SFI truth, authority or preference |
| Agent2Agent (A2A) | discover/interoperate with external agent systems through declared agent capabilities/tasks | A2A does not replace SFI agent governance or provenance |
| controlled agent harnesses / durable runtimes | optional executor substrate for bounded long-running tasks | do not replace the existing institutional cycle unless measured runtime failure justifies migration |
| Cedar or Open Policy Agent | candidates for an externalized policy-decision point for typed authorization | authorization remains distinct from epistemic admissibility |
| W3C PROV | interoperability/export vocabulary for existing lineage and provenance | internal canonical identity is not rewritten to fit PROV |
| OpenTelemetry | operational traces, spans and runtime correlation | telemetry is not evidence and does not upgrade epistemic class |
| NIST AI RMF / TEVV-Athlon | external evaluation crosswalk for real-world outcome and risk testing | external frameworks do not auto-certify SFI or replace CPRT/Field return contracts |

Adapters must preserve the canonical SFI rule:

`EXTERNAL STANDARD SUPPORT != EXTERNAL STANDARD AUTHORITY OVER SFI SEMANTICS`.

## Gate

No future SFI component may claim `SFI WILL`, `SFI DESIRE`, `MUTUAL CONSENT`, `JOINT PREFERENCE`, `SELECTED-FUTURE PROBABILITY`, `ATTRACTOR GRAVITY`, or `BASIN POPULATION` merely from LLM output, agent execution, repeated memory, rhetorical coherence, or behavioral consistency.

Such claims require a separately versioned and governed epistemic/mathematical contract.
